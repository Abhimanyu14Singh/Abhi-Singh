How to use Truss Lab

  1. Draw joints — pick the Joint tool and click the grid (clicks snap to the grid).
  2. Connect members — pick Member, click a joint, then click another.
  3. Add supports — pick Support and click a joint to cycle pin → roller → none. You need at least 3 reaction components for stability.
  4. Apply loads — pick Load, click a joint, then type the load components in the Properties tab.
  5. Read the story — the Insights tab explains which member attracts the most force, which one controls the deflection, and how to fix it efficiently (Baker's virtual-work view).
  6. Pick the joint you care about — in Insights, choose which joint's deflection (and which direction) to break down. The purple “1” arrow on the drawing is the virtual unit load.
  7. Experiment — select a member, give it an AISC shape or change its area, and re-solve. In a determinate truss forces stay put but deflections change; in an indeterminate one the forces redistribute too.
  8. Move around — scroll to zoom, drag empty space (Select tool) to pan, Fit view to re-centre. Drag a joint onto another to join them.

Keys: S select · N joint · M member · R support · L load · D delete · F fit · Esc cancel · Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo/redo · Del delete selection

The method under the hood

Member forces and joint displacements come from the direct stiffness method. The deflection of the most-displaced joint is then decomposed with the unit-load (virtual work) method, δ = Σ N·n·L/(EA), so every member's individual contribution is visible. The two methods agree — that agreement is the whole point.
